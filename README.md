# Atelier

**A creative instrument for iPad.**

---

Atelier is not a notes app. It's not a sketchpad. It's not a word processor.

It's the table in your studio at 2am — where a half-finished lyric sits next to a rough sketch, where an audio waveform bleeds into an architect's measurement, where the best ideas happen in the margins between disciplines.

---

## The Feel

Every element on the canvas is a physical object. Pick it up. Feel it lift. Drag it across the surface and watch it follow your finger with weight — not instantly, not robotically, but with the resistance of something real. Drop it, and it overshoots, settles, finds its place. Like a stamp pressed too hard. Like a page landing on a desk.

Swipe between canvases like turning pages in a journal you've kept for years. The edges peek. The paper resists at the boundaries. Pinch out, and your canvases become cards on a shelf — objects you can see, hold, rearrange.

The waveform isn't a static image. Drag your finger across it and scrub through sound. The bars lean toward your touch, responding to proximity like iron filings near a magnet.

Over time, the canvas remembers where you've been. Faint traces of every touch accumulate — a patina of interaction, like the worn brass of a door handle that's been turned ten thousand times.

---

## Three Canvases, Three Lives

Atelier ships with three pre-populated canvases that demonstrate what this instrument can become:

**Late Night Session** — A musician's working notes. A waveform from demo tape 03 sits alongside scattered thoughts about chord progressions and string arrangements. The silence between the notes matters more than the notes themselves.

**Chapter Opening** — A writer's draft in progress. First attempt, editorial note in the margin ("Cut the first sentence"), second draft below. The canvas is the conversation between drafts.

**Courtyard Measurements** — An architect's site visit. Hand-sketched floor plan with dimension lines, column marks, and a burnt orange arrow tracing the path of afternoon sun. The measurements are approximate. The light is extraordinary.

---

## The Principles

Atelier is built on nine interaction pillars — **Grain, Warmth, Patina, Weight, Friction, Fluid, Tactility, Resistance, Silence** — that push the iPad toward something more analog, more honest, more human than the glass rectangle it pretends to be.

Every spring constant is tuned by hand. Every haptic is authored, not defaulted. The grain overlay is procedural noise, not an image. The parchment is warm because screens are cold and someone needs to fix that.

There is no dark mode. The parchment is the identity.
